# Daggerheart – Session 2 Notes

**Title:** _The Road Through Nightveil_  
**Theme:** Trust and shadows. The past weighs on the road. Shared hardship binds what Haven's cracks cannot.  
**Setting:** The caravan from [[Stolen Fen]] to the border barracks under Nightveil's strange twilight, then on to [[Monks Eliegh]], a place frozen in stone and prayer.

---

## Departure from Stolen Fen

Stolen Fen was dimly lit. Lanterns hooded. Fires banked low. Peat smoke and damp reeds hung in the air. Traders moved quietly, voices hushed, as if the night itself were listening. The Clanks in the fields stood motionless, work paused until dawn.

At the edge of the settlement a wagon waited. Wheels reinforced for marshy ground. A weary driver with a lantern hung low. A pair of oxen snorted and stamped, uneasy in the gathering dark.

Before departure, Captain Thorne found [[Sabriel Arden|Sabriel]]. Regret sat in the old captain's voice.

"Arden. About yesterday… I should've been ready. I let the drink get the better of me, and I let you down. Won't happen again. You've got my word."

Earlier Sabriel had spoken with [[Delilah Goldtooth]], asking if she could get a message to [[Ambrose Salecia|Ambrose]] in the city. The corruption was stronger. He was not sure how sanitized the reports were that reached Ambrose. The last Bloodwing seen had been around three metres. He suspected they were growing more aggressive.

Thorne offered a final warning.

"Nightveil's no time for travel. But if you must, keep your eyes sharp. The dark has a way of hiding more than shadows."

[[Snicket Surefoot|Snicket]] also spoke with Delilah and other merchants, fishing for anything more. Nothing yet. They would be in contact if that changed.

[[Fern Wingsteria|Fern]] and [[Fianna Fernwhisperer|Fianna]] wandered the markets. Clothes, herbs, sweets not tasted before. Small comforts for the road.

[[Gnirth]] tried his Fungril connection to reach others of his kin. Nothing answered. Other than Fern, the network was silent. The quiet felt heavier than it should.

---

## The shrine and the questions

Before they left, the wagon driver stepped away from the cart. [[Harlen]], mid-forties, weathered, missing two fingers. Short of patience, but with time for formalities. He offered a soft prayer at a shrine to [[Qui’Gar, Whispered Sweetness for a Thorny End|Qui'Gar]].

The shrine stood near the wagon yard, draped in thorny vines and wilted flowers. A clay bowl of honey sat at its base, buzzing faintly with insects. Locals bowed their heads as they passed, whispering for safe passage through the fen.

Fianna and Fern opened up to Snicket and Sabriel about the gods and the faint divinities. The party debated it openly. Who the shrine was for. Why anyone would still believe. Fern noticed the offering, the clay bowl of honey, differed from what she had seen before in her own worship of Qui'Gar. The questions hung without easy answers.

---

## On the road through Nightveil

The wagon creaked along the narrow track. Its lantern cast a pale circle of light. Beyond it the world was swallowed by Nightveil, the strange twilight where the stars burned brighter and the air felt thick, as if holding its breath. Reeds whispered in the wind. Every splash of water sounded too close.

After some time a low rumbling fog swept the land. Fianna had never seen its like and worried. It seemed harmless, though it cut visibility to no more than far distance. The mist felt alive, carrying whispers that might have been wind or something else.

Gnirth tried his control of water. He parted the mist and opened the path again. More important, it revealed a hooded figure ahead, standing among the reeds, still as a statue. Face hidden. Gaze palpable.

After some discussion Sabriel called out, "Friend or foe?" The figure vanished into the dark. Snicket scoured the spot. Footsteps led to where the figure had stood. Nothing led away. Only a single copper coin remained, thorns around the rim and an eye etched in the middle. The party took it for some kind of group sigil. None of them had seen it before.

---

## The sick bear

Soon after, a ragged cry of pain and anguish split the air. Fianna reached it first, driven by her care for creatures and by the memory of what should have happened with the Bloodwings.

In the thicket she found a large brown bear, its extremities corrupted by a black, oozing substance. She had seen this before with her companion Brindle. The sight went through her. She spoke to the creature. It asked only that she help remove the pain.

Not knowing how to lift the curse, she called the party for support and comforted the bear, hands gentle even as tears ran. Snicket and Sabriel worked together to deal a fatal blow, weapons finding the heart with merciful precision. The bear's eyes closed with something like gratitude. In death the corruption seemed to recede, leaving only the memory of what it had been.

Gnirth used his gift to read the mind of the just-parted bear, seeking what it had feared. The bear had been outcast from its group for fear of exposing them to the infection. Alone in a foreign forest, scared and without understanding. Only pain, and loss of control.

They returned to the wagon and reached the barracks. There a ragtag group of cadets were being trained within an inch of their lives by a hardass dwarf named [[Valerius D'sousza|Valerius]].

"ARE YOU BLOCKING OR ARE YOU PARRYING?!?!?" His voice cracked across the training yard, sharp and unrelenting.

---

## The barracks and Valerius

After catching up, and some traded insults, Valerius was glad to see his old friend. He had been tasked with training the new recruits. Upper Haven feared an upcoming assault from [[Fanewick]] in retaliation. Rumours and murmuring of discord were circulating about the damage Haven had done.

The party asked to stay until Sunnar came again. In return they would help train the recruits, train themselves, and take on small side tasks. In that stretch they reached Level 2.

Just before leaving, Gnirth reached through his power once more and found someone in the kitchens cleaning up after the officers.

She revealed herself as a migrant called [[Tybella]]. She felt she had been rescued by the Havenites, even if not directly. She was from the Blue Bell Tribe of Fungril, deep to the south of the Witherwild. They had early knowledge of the tar-like substance taking over creatures in the wild. Its source, she said, was an explosion around the time the Sowing Eye was stolen and permanent spring settled across the land. Whether those threads were one knot remained an open, unsettling question.

---

## Journey to Monks Eliegh

On the road again, Sabriel said it was another two or three days to Monks Eliegh. The travel was quiet and uneventful, night and day. Under his direction it took little time. He knew the paths.

What they found was a town of frozen souls, people trapped in stone during their last actions. Most had been at prayer, pointing toward the chapel. That chapel was tended by a sole person, [[Mathias Vanta]].

---

## The chapel of stone prayers

The road wound down into a hollow where Monks Eliegh once stood. Houses silent, doors ajar, roofs sagging under dust and time. A red serpent had been painted on the old watchtower, Haven's mark of a town lost to the Sickness. The air was dry and heavy with stone and incense.

In the streets they saw them. Statues of men, women, and children, frozen mid-prayer, faces turned toward the chapel at the town's heart. The silence was absolute, broken only by wind through broken shutters.

The chapel doors creaked open on rows of stone figures kneeling in prayer. Faces worn with grief. Hands clasped. Eyes on the altar. Dust lay thick on the pews. One thing stood out. A single candle burned at the altar, flame steady in the still air. At the altar a gaunt priest knelt among the stone figures, his voice a low murmur of endless prayer.

A kindly, gentle voice reassured the party. Mathias was glad to see Sabriel safe, and concerned about the burden he carried. He also revealed that a halfling matching the description of Fianna's father had come to him seeking help in matters revolving around a god, [[Nikta, the Great Owl]].

That was something Mathias could not help with, but he had given shelter and food where he could. Last he heard, the man was heading to [[Haven]], though Mathias was not sure he would find what he sought there.

Sabriel's old house still stood, better kept than the rest of the ruined village.

Snicket spoke privately with Sabriel about the mysterious note she had received, knowing he had ties to [[Kreil Durn|Kreil]]. The talk was hushed.

Gnirth tried to commune with one of the statues and instead cracked its head, shattering it. Fear was rolled. The sound of breaking stone echoed through the silent chapel.

Fern placed a memento on another statue. The body crumbled under her touch. Dust and fragments fell away, as if the stone were too fragile to bear the weight of memory.

Sabriel confided in Mathias about his endless pilgrimages. Mathias urged him to see this as a chance for change.

"Even a small stone can turn a river in time."

Sabriel revealed to the group that he was one of the few who returned from the war, after Snicket had embellished tales of his past. The admission sat heavy with survivors' guilt.

Gnirth admitted his accident with the statue to Mathias, who accepted it with quiet sadness. A priest who had learned to find peace in imperfection.

Donning his old armor, Sabriel prepared to leave. Snicket then shared the contents of the note, and the group agreed to travel to the Old Weir.

---

## Kreil's message

The session ended with Mathias speaking to Snicket. He had been asked to pass on a letter to someone matching her exact description, told she would arrive within a few days, around now.

The letter read: "I have been observing you through the network. Your help will be needed to avoid a great threat. Meet me at the Old Weir by next Nightveil. Bring only a trusted few."

The Old Weir lay west of here, no more than a few hours' travel. Very much out of the way, and a perfect place for secrecy, abandoned many years before.

The session ended there. A letter in Snicket's hands. The Old Weir a few hours west. Answers promised, and whatever came with them.
