# Daggerheart – Session 4 Notes

**Title:** _Echoes of the Weir_  
**Theme:** Recovery under pressure. Fragile alliances. Personal quests pull as Nightveil draws near.  
**Setting:** Aftermath of the ambush at the Old Weir, the road back through the fen, and preparations for [[Alula]].

---

## Interrogation at the Weir

Dust still hung in the cave of the Old Weir from the collapse. Motes caught the light and fell slow. Outside, the fen hummed. Blood and charred stone hung in the air.

The hogtied prisoner, an Inferni zealot, glared up with defiance. Faint black veins pulsed beneath his skin. He spoke to [[Fern Wingsteria|Fern]], [[Fianna Fernwhisperer|Fianna]], and [[Gnirth]] with an unsettling calm. They were "unlike the others," he said. Why would nature-bound souls ally with Haven's soldiers?

That taunt drew them into interrogation. The balance skewed fast. The zealot seemed to learn more about them than they did about him. Their questions told him their motives.

Gnirth tried intimidation. He produced a jar of Witherwild tar, its oily surface shimmering like a bruise, and let the threat speak for him. The Inferni did not flinch. [[Sabriel Arden|Sabriel]] pressed for the barracks. Let [[Valerius D'sousza|Valerius]] handle it properly. The prisoner's eyes lit with a spark that felt wrong, as if Haven's cells suited him. Doubt moved through the group.

---

## Contacting Tybella

With tensions rising, Fern and Gnirth linked into the Fungril network. Unseen threads stretched through soil and root toward the barracks kitchens. A faint hum pricked the skin. The air smelled of damp earth and spores.

[[Tybella]] answered. She confirmed no expected visitors beyond new recruits, most already rerouted to [[Haven]]. Together, the three spoke openly: where the party was, who was present, and the plan to move on toward Alula once the prisoner was dealt with.

When Fern and Gnirth tried to pull back, the link did not release cleanly. It tugged. Tybella seemed to linger a heartbeat too long before fading. Gnirth felt it first, a chill certainty that the channel was not secure. Someone could have been listening.

---

## The false tooth

Even as the communion faded, Sabriel noticed the prisoner working his jaw. Tongue searching the back of his mouth. His face tightened with grim resolve.

Sabriel forced the assassin's mouth open. [[Snicket Surefoot|Snicket]] slipped in and plucked out the hidden object: a poisoned false tooth. The Inferni had chosen death over capture. Whatever confidence he had shown about the barracks had been a mask. He had never meant to arrive alive.

They bound him more tightly. Snicket palmed the tooth. The cave took the last of the scuffle's echoes.

---

## The split

Nightveil loomed only days away. There was no time to ferry the prisoner to [[Monks Eliegh]], then to the barracks, and return again. The party split.

Fianna took horse shape and bore the prisoner with Sabriel riding guard. The pace was brutal. Fianna pushed through the strain, her breath fogging in short, sharp bursts. They made the barracks without incident and requested a private meeting with Valerius.

Behind closed doors, Sabriel shared [[Kreil Durn|Kreil]]'s revelations: the Fanewraith's plan to take the Sowing Eye, the ambush, the assassin's fanatic resolve. Valerius was strapped for resources with recruit rotations, but he still found a way to help. He supplied armor repair kits, one per party member. On a long rest, each kit could upgrade an armor piece to Tier 2. Single use.

They asked about Tybella. Valerius snorted that he hoped she could be trusted. She was feeding his troops. He confirmed she had already prepared a cell. With that, Sabriel and Fianna began the return to Monks Eliegh. The road stayed quiet.

Meanwhile Fern, Snicket, and Gnirth retraced the trail to Monks Eliegh on foot. Without Sabriel they traveled alert and careful, letting silence and the fen's small noises fill the gaps between them.

---

## Fulg among the stones

Midway, the trio found a cairn by the roadside. New. Precariously balanced. A single stone missing from the top. Fern searched the brush and found a perfect match: smooth, cool, just the right weight.

As she set it in place, the stack vibrated with a low, resonant hum. Stones tumbled and rolled, not collapsing but reforming, clicking into a new shape with a forward roll that ended in a seated, rocky figure.

[[Fulg, Stacker of Stones|Fulg]] regarded them with patient calm.

He thanked Fern for restoring balance. His voice, like a gravel slide, spoke of the careful equilibrium of the world and how the Witherwild had thrown its steps into chaos. He did not fix blame. He only named imbalance. Coordinated action, he warned, could so easily become disorder. He would not push the scales further.

Asked about other faint divinities, Fulg spoke of Ikla the Sky Painter, tired of painting the same spring sky over and over. Of Oove, the Watcher of the Night Bloom, whom Ikla favored. Of [[Bolgin, Bringer of the Black Marsh|Bolgin]], long silent, now impossible to reach. He admitted he wandered away from many waters. The rivers felt tainted. Perhaps connected to Bolgin, though he would not speculate.

Snicket balked at the sight, skeptical and disbelieving. Gnirth tried to lance Fulg with an insult, only to be calmly named "no different than a flower," a line that struck deeper than expected.

Fulg offered what aid he could. Not action. Not advice. Guidance. Follow the cairns if you become lost.

The road beyond stayed quiet. The trio pressed on.

---

## Reunion at Monks Eliegh

By evening the group reconvened in Monks Eliegh's chapel. Statue shadows reached across the floor. [[Mathias Vanta|Mathias]]' candle burned with its steady flame.

They exchanged news. The prisoner was secured. Repair kits had been procured. Nightveil crept closer. With the road to Alula ahead, they chose a long rest, mending armor, quieting breath, steadying hands.

Dust settled. The air held the dry, clean scent of stone and wax.

---

## Fern's communion

Fern drew her circle with care. She reached not outward but downward, into the memory of Bolgin, into the moment after Sabriel cut him down.

The first sensation was taste. Gasoline, sharp and suffocating, poured over her skin and down her throat. She gagged and coughed thick tar that clung like a second tongue.

The second sensation came with earth. Bitter, mossy bile crept up and stuck to her skin, a slick, gritty coating that would not wash away.

The third sensation dragged her under. She fell into a black ocean of tar, swimming through treacle beneath a sky she did not recognize. Strange stars burned and moved in auroral currents, alive, restless, beautiful.

A small island broke the horizon. Fern crawled onto its shore, lungs burning, and saw a toad perched on the ridge. Its eyes were deep wells, black holes drinking the sky. She felt the draw before she understood it. Then the world seized her and ripped her free of her body.

The voice came from everywhere: vast, cold, and absolute. "WHO DARES SUMMON ME."

Fern tried to speak. She tried to say she was there for balance, for answers, to stop the Witherwild's spread. The voice cut through her plea.

"YOU CANNOT STOP WHAT IS HAPPENING, NOR WHAT IS COMING. IT HAS ALREADY BEEN SET IN MOTION. I WILL RETURN AND SHOW THE SO-CALLED GOD KILLERS HOW MORTAL THEY REALLY ARE."

The universe pressed down. The island shattered into light. Fern's consciousness slammed back into her body. Her eyes rolled white. Black tar bled from her mouth and eyes. The summoning circle broke.

Silence. The chapel. The candle. The stone figures watching. Fern collapsed unconscious, the echo of that voice lingering like a bruise on the air.

The session ended there. Prisoner secured. Repair kits in hand. Nightveil close. And Fern broken open by a divinity that had answered.
