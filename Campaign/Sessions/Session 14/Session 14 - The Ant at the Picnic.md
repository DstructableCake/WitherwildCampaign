# Daggerheart – Session 14 Notes

**Title:** _The Ant at the Picnic_  
**Theme:** Guilt, grief, and the cost of cure. The river tastes of metal, and every answer Lyra gives asks for more life than it returns.  
**Setting:** The Riverwalker camp on familiar hunting grounds, then [[Elder Lyra Willowbrook|Elder Lyra]]'s hut, where a sermon that is not quite a sermon lays bare what has gone wrong between mortal soil and spirit realm.

---

## A known tradition

[[Fern Wingsteria|Fern]] watched the others twitch and sweat as the rite took them. This was no accident. The River Folk knew the tradition. They stayed calm, caught the bodies as they slumped, and let sleep take them.

---

## Hard counsel by the fire

[[Fianna Fernwhisperer|Fianna]] spoke with [[Theodore Underbrush|Theo]] about what makes a person good, and about the guilt of lives she had taken. Theo told her the choices mattered more than the comfort of them. Even a deed that felt right in the moment could cast a long shadow. The dead would stay in the back of her mind. She would have to learn from them, stay true to herself, and do what she thought was right rather than what was easy.

[[Sabriel Arden|Sabriel]] spoke with [[Silas]] about [[Tavore Paran|Tavore]], how she had lost her husband and child, and about Alula in careful, incomplete strokes. He spoke of his own family as well. Silas said even the sternest mind could change, though the odds might look impossible. Sometimes telling someone they were wrong was not enough. They had to live the lesson, the way parents know their children must stumble before they understand.

Silas had sent a scouting party looking for unfamiliar faces. They were due back in the next few days. Sabriel mentioned Haven's two rotations against mutated wood and beasts. This stretch of forest held little cursed soil, he said, which made the river's corruption all the stranger.

---

## Faces around the camp

A human ranger taught youngsters rope and knots. A Katari bard worked a drum. A faun druid chanted at the water. Elder Lyra had already retired to her hut.

An old Galapa spoke of apocalypse, angels, and demons. Ramblings of age, or something the smoke left ringing in his mouth.

Fianna found [[Kesra Thornwade]], the young faun druid, moving her hands slowly over the current, folding water between her palms and letting it spread. The river was getting worse. Once she had cleansed it around once a day. Now she needed three, and even then a metallic taste remained. The pollution had used to come from the east. Now it came from the north. Something upstream was poisoning the flow.

About a year ago, Elder Lyra had brought the cure to the group. Kesra did not know what it was. River maps marked Kesra's skin in tattoo. Fianna copied them down.

[[Snicket Surefoot|Snicket]] spoke with [[Brenn Mudsong]], a Katari bard whose family had been travelling musicians. She asked after [[Elowen]]. Brenn knew a song of an old woman who flickered from place to place, luring travellers with minty brew, kept company by a large-nosed beast. He sounded agitated that the sky's palette had not changed in months. Perhaps Ikla had run out of inspiration, or of choice, when painting sunrise and sunset. Asked if Elowen had enemies, he was baffled. Who would make an enemy of such a lovely old lady? Snicket gave him a dramatised telling of the fight at Elowen's hollowed home and of Demetrius's death, enough for a song to grow from.

Sabriel sought out the halfling Seraf. What he asked went unfinished.

---

## Three questions in the hut

Lyra greeted them with a twinkle in her eye and a soft smile. As they settled she asked three things. Were they too tired for the smoke? Did anyone fear enclosed spaces? Would anyone object if she called this a sermon?

Snicket asked if Lyra would mind laughter. Lyra said no.

Metal rose at the back of their tongues. Lyra told them she could not give a path to heaven. She was far too small for that. She saw the world as an ant looking up at a picnic, watching things move beyond anyone's full grasp.

Sabriel asked after a cure for Haven's sickness, how to get more red flowers, and why only the red ones worked. The flowers, she said, were a gift from the spirit realm. The red ones held a living connection to that place. Each bloom that grew took life essence from both the mortal world and the spirit realm. Perpetual growth had driven the flowers hard. Larger and larger fields would be needed for the same yield. The more you grew, the more the soil drained, the more destruction followed to make room for more flowers, and the cycle worsened.

[[Shun'Aush, the Granit Ophid|Shun'Aush]]'s body after the battle had left spirit and flesh severed, the mortal husk breaking down across ages. Nature's balance had been wrong since then. The same wound marked [[Bolgin, Bringer of the Black Marsh|Bolgin]] after the battle of the bog. Spirit cut from body. Body left to taint the world.

Fianna asked how to restore balance. Lyra said the balance of growing and sowing could be restored by returning the eyes to [[Nikta, the Great Owl|Nikta]]. For Faint Divinities already felled, balance might return if their spirits were put back into mortal bodies, or summoned into new ones. To remove them completely, they would have to be banished to the spirit realm, then hunted in their home world and defeated a final time.

She spoke of the Spire in Fanewick as an old pilgrimage road. [[Rohkin, the Wandering Horn]] seemed to be leading herds somewhere toward the salt marshes.

The stuff that cured the river was the essence of life itself. Lyra could commune between the human and spiritual worlds, and each time she did she gave up a little of her own life for the components.

Sabriel asked how to reach the spirit world. Lyra said that knowledge was too dangerous to share. Fianna pointed out that Fern could already commune with spirit, and asked whether Lyra might share it with her instead.

The session ended there, in the close air of Lyra's hut, with metal still on their tongues and the question of the spirit road left open between them.
