# Daggerheart – Session 8 Notes

**Title:** _The Lute, the Horn and the Missing Father_  
**Theme:** Recovery and first steps into true Fanewick. Who the wild belongs to is an open question. The river remembers every footprint and washes none away.  
**Setting:** The days after Serpents Hope falls. Back roads and river paths leave Haven's ordered fields for the Riverwalkers' Hunting Grounds and the treetop stronghold of [[Alula]].

---

## Scars in the soil

The days after the siege settled into a numb, working silence. Warehouses stood patched but wounded, three walls rebuilt in timber that did not quite match. Lady's Veil bundles sat stacked and tarped, guarded like cargo waiting for the road. Beyond them the fields told a harsher story. Wide stretches of soil stained grey-black where the ooze had sunk. Once-white rows reduced to ragged scars. Workers moved with shovels and sacks, clearing bodies, breaking hardened roots, avoiding the worst patches.

Quartermaster Brill's voice had gone hoarse from shouting. Steward Vel moved quieter, helping children sort what belongings had survived. The party took their long rest in the shadow of what had been lost.

Before they went deeper into Fanewick they still had work. Messages to send. Supplies to gather. Questions that might shape the road.

---

## Messages and preparations

[[Snicket Surefoot|Snicket]] found quiet enough to encode warnings. To Delilah Goldtooth she sent word of the farm's fall. Raise red potion prices. Limit sales. Increase security. Supplies would be scarce. Serpents Hope was only the start.

Her second message went to [[Kreil Durn|Kreil Durn]]. A description of the elderly woman they had met at dawn. [[Elowen]], with her mole companion Demetrius. Location. Demeanor. Her strange view of the tainted creatures. In her trade even quiet encounters could matter.

[[Sabriel Arden|Sabriel]] wrote his own warnings. To Ambrose Salecia he reported the Leshen at the farms, a guiding force behind the corruption, coordinated daylight attacks that spoke of strategy beyond mindless beasts.

To Mathias Vanta he mirrored Snicket's scarcity warning, so the priest would know to be careful. Letters as ties to a world they were leaving.

---

## Trading and gathering

Traders still came and went from Serpents Hope. The market was thin, resources stretched by the attack, but bartering and goodwill from the defense opened doors.

They took what they could use. A Fire Jar. A Glamour Stone. A recipe for Minor Stamina Potions on weathered parchment. A Suspended Rod. An Arcane Cloak. A Skeleton Key warm to the touch.

Among the traders they also heard talk of faint divinities in Fanewick. Sylvan, the Songweaver, music made visible, bard or bird depending on the teller. Vaelis, the Forgotten Echo, mist that remembered what others forgot. Rohkin, the Wandering Horn, an ethereal stag whose antlers reached for the sky, a spirit who demanded strength and respect from those who sought him.

---

## Fern's communion and the deceptive vision

[[Fern Wingsteria|Fern]] found a quiet corner and drew her circle with care. The dark stains beneath her eyes pulsed faintly. The last communion still sat close. Cosmic entity. Black ocean. A voice that had threatened return. This time she sought guidance for [[Fianna Fernwhisperer|Fianna]], not answers about the corruption.

She reached through the hedge. "Holy Nikta, one of your acolytes, Fianna, has asked me to ask you whether the grove is prepared for another attempt of an attack against you."

The answer was gentler than before. Almost too perfect. Fern saw Fianna's grove, the Whitefeather Circle of the Feathersong Kin. Soft golden light. Birds in harmony. Flowers in perfect cycles. People moving in a peace untouched by the spread across Fanewick. Everything fine. No trouble at all.

Something nagged. The perfection was too complete. It lacked the small flaws that make a place real. Still the message was clear. The grove was safe, prepared, in no danger.

---

## Leaving Haven's shadow

Messages sent, supplies gathered, they prepared to leave. Sun still high, they secured a horse and wagon. Fianna offered to pull or transform if needed. Farewells to Quartermaster Brill and the survivors, then the road north.

Familiar territory at first. Neat white flower rows giving way to taller grass and knotty shrubs. The packed earth thinned to wagon ruts, then a track, then something more habit than path.

Along the way they found remnants of the battle's reach. Corpses of creatures from the mass combat at Serpents Hope. A Brambleweb Crawler partially dissolved in a drainage ditch, thorny legs curled, black ooze still seeping. A Flameburst Firefly carcass, chitin cracked and scorched. Glasswing Moth fragments like broken glass, wings still faintly lit. Creatures that should not have been this far from the farms. Driven, or fleeing something worse.

Soon the road went sparse and the wilds took over. Trees closed in. Vines hung like curtains. The river's hush swelled from murmur to constant presence, just out of sight. Less smoke and sweat. More leaf mold and water. The line where Haven's order ended and Fanewick began.

A border stone rose from a hummock ahead, half-buried, half-overgrown. One face bore the faint chipped sigil of the Stone Serpent, scales nearly lost under moss. The other held small pebbles and twisted metal. Offerings from travelers with no banners. The threshold.

---

## Riverwalker ground

The forest thickened along the river. Branches knit until the sky was more suggestion than sight. Faint paths appeared and vanished. Deer trails. Old hunter tracks. Roots worn smooth by generations of feet.

Then woodsmoke too steady to be wild. Roasting meat. Crushed herbs. A thin thread of smoke through the canopy.

They took another step. A quiet voice said, "Enough."

A Wickling man stood half in shadow, half on a fallen log above the path. Leather armor in forest tones. Bow unstrung but close. One bare foot on a carved spirit cairn of river stones. His eyes moved from Sabriel's armor to Fern's spores to [[Gnirth]]'s odd veins, then to Fianna's antlers and Snicket's scars.

"Riverwalker ground," he said. Quiet. Precise. Rarely a wasted word. "You walk it loud. State your hunt, strangers."

Silas "Thornfoot." Lean elf Wickling. Braided hair bound with river reeds. Thorn tattoos spiraling up his calves. Bare feet hardened by a lifetime on the forest floor.

They explained themselves. Travelers seeking Alula. Survivors of Serpents Hope. Allies to those who respected the land. Silas listened as a guardian listens, weighing not only words but how they spoke of land, beasts, and kin.

Talk turned to how hunting had grown harder, how the water had been tainted. His wariness eased a little. He told them to speak with Elder Lyra Willowbrook at their small camp. Answers, maybe. Guidance for the road.

---

## The Riverwalker campfire

The camp emerged from the trees as if grown there. Hide tents blending into roots. Racks of drying fish and meat in a clearing. A central fire. Camouflaged figures between the trunks. Balanced stone cairns marking spirit paths.

Elder Lyra Willowbrook was older, weathered, her traveler's cloak patched with different clan patterns. Herb bundles and charms rattled as she moved. She welcomed them with a storyteller's cadence, gentle, low humor.

Around the fire they heard what endless spring had done to Riverwalker life. Herds no longer rested. Animals always moving. Calves year-round, many born wrong. Extra limbs. Wrong colors. Strange behavior. Plants wrong too, some blooming until the soil gave out, others refusing to grow. Spirits restless. Rohkin's omens confused. River spirits distressed.

Of Rohkin she spoke with deep knowledge. "If you seek to heal beasts, speak with Rohkin. But know this: he is not the only spirit the herds listen to, not anymore."

When she turned to Gnirth she studied him carefully. "You carry the taste of a drowned place," she said. "Tideveil, perhaps. The water there turned thick and did not want to move." The words struck hard. A thread of memory he could not quite hold.

Most important for Fianna, Lyra confirmed it. A traveling man with a lute had passed a few days prior, a small horned creature in tow. The description matched [[Theodore Underbrush|Theodore]]. Alive. Close. Hope and fear together.

Lyra asked them to cleanse the water upstream on the way to Alula, and gave them what she knew of the place. Enough to hold the taint a while. Not forever.

As the fire died to embers the Riverwalkers showed a narrow path deeper into Fanewick, upstream. "The path grows harder from here," Silas warned. "The river remembers what it used to be, and what it's become. Watch your step."

---

## Qui'Gar's gift

Leaving the camp along the river, Fern spotted a small overgrown shrine tucked in a briar patch at the water's edge.

A shrine to Qui'Gar, Whispered Sweetness for a Thorny End. The same faint divinity she had been leaving cake for. Recent offerings. Crumbs fresh and old. In a clay bowl at the base, a single slice of cake. From its center a pale green stem with delicate thorns pulsed with faint bioluminescence. Tiny as a thumb. Alive beyond ordinary growth. When Fern approached the thorns unfurled a little and the light brightened, as if it knew her.

A whisper on the wind, soft as thorns on silk. *"A gift for the one who remembers the sweetness of endings... Tend it well, and it may grow to serve rather than consume."*

---

## The tainted ford

Further upriver, as the sun set and Nightveil's perpetual twilight claimed the sky, they found a stone crossing where black tar visibly tainted the water. Dark and thick in patches. Stones stained the same as Serpents Hope.

Across the ford something caught moonlight. A glint that should not have been there. First they had to cross.

Together they blessed and purified the water. Magics and faiths enough for the corruption to recede a while. Clearer water long enough to cross. A stopgap, as Lyra had warned. Enough to put them closer to Alula. Sounds of the treetop village already audible ahead.

---

## Evidence of struggle

On the far side they tracked the reflection. What they found hit Fianna hard.

Remnants of a lute, strings catching moon like silver thread. Broken beyond simple repair, discarded in the undergrowth. Beside it, under leaves, a broken-off section of antler. Thumb-sized. Unmistakable.

She knew both at once. Her father's lute. Theodore's. Songs of Nikta and balance. The antler was Brindle's, her corrupted fox companion taken by the Witherwild. They had been together. They had been here.

Broken sticks. Dark blood on the ground. Tracks into the bush toward Alula. Ambush, fight, or flight.

Speaking to the trees, Fianna learned what they had witnessed. Days prior, a man and an animal ambushed by dark creatures. One eight or nine feet tall. Two of ordinary build. One smaller, like a halfling. Inferni assassins of the kind they had faced at the Old Weir, working with something larger.

Theodore and Brindle together. Attacked. Brindle injured. The lute damaged in the struggle. Captured or fled in danger. Close either way. Needing help.

---

## First glimpse of Alula

The path climbed. Roots became stairs in places. The river slipped further below until its noise was a distant hush. Older trees. Broader trunks. Branches interlocking in dizzying webs.

They crested a final rise and the world opened.

Above, woven into the canopy, a village of platforms and rope-bridges, huts and halls and hanging gardens. Lanterns of bloom clusters swayed, soft light on bark and leaf. Walkways trunk to trunk, some barely visible, others crowded with distant figures like ants in the air.

Lower down, smaller platforms served as guard posts and waystations. Wicklings and others stood watch with spears and bows. Alula spread over three core layers. SunLofts in the treetops for council, scholars, observatories, government and enforcement. Heartwalk in the mid-level for markets, stalls, traders, and houses, dense and favela-like. Shadowreach in the undercanopy for refugee quarters, smugglers' dens, and black markets, thinner, wilds concealing entrances.

Enemy ground. The Fanewraith's location unknown. Potentially dozens of assassins and spies. Careful steps. Careful trust.

They carried a few useful facts. Haven remnants would draw hostility. Sabriel would need caution. Snicket had a delivery for Mr Tilde, a Galapa somewhere in the city. They knew how the original assassins looked and dressed, and some names. They knew the serpent coin marked those in the network.

At the path's edge a sign read "Welcome to Alula." Below it, a bowl and a note.

Minty soup remnants, still warm. The note in a familiar hand.

"Really looking forward to seeing you all again, I really enjoyed our last meet. E"

Elowen. The woman from dawn. Meal shared. Kindness offered. She had been here. She had left this. She had known they were coming.

The session ended there. Alula above them, promise and threat both. Theodore and Brindle's fate unknown. In Fanewick, little was quite what it seemed.
