# Daggerheart – Session 7 Notes

**Title:** _The Siege of Serpents Hope - Part 2_  
**Theme:** Desperate defense. Escalating corruption. Dawn shows what the choices cost.  
**Setting:** Daybreak at [[Serpents Hope]] Farms. Warehouses, civilian quarters, and awakening fields as the corruption births a guardian of twisted roots and bone.

---

## The battle continues

The siege was already underway. Chaos spread across the farm. The three Flameburst Fireflies lay dead, their explosive ends leaving scorch marks and the smell of charred chitin. Only the Briarback Boar remained, hide bristling with thorns as it charged through the smouldering wreckage of the civilian quarters.

[[Sabriel Arden|Sabriel]] took command. Warehouses under attack. The Wither Touched Stag already through three walls. He made the call. [[Snicket Surefoot|Snicket]] would support [[Fern Wingsteria|Fern]] and [[Fianna Fernwhisperer|Fianna]] against the Bloodwings and the stag before the warehouses and the Lady's Veil flowers took more damage. [[Gnirth]] would take the boar alone. Sabriel himself would enter the mist and hold whatever waited inside.

They sprinted across the farm, boots pounding, breath sharp. The run left them winded. No time to rest. Sabriel broke off toward the fog dust cloud over the eastern fields. Whatever hid in that mist, he would hold it while the others secured the warehouses.

---

## Gnirth's fight

Gnirth faced the Briarback Boar alone. He tried to sear it with blazing hands, working through the thorny hide to the flesh beneath. Heat rose from his palms. Burning hair and charred meat filled the air.

The boar bolted through a fence. Thorns snagged Gnirth's clothing and pinned him to its side. Barbs tore fabric and skin as it dragged him through mud and dirt.

The world blurred. The boar crashed through a building and set it ablaze. Flames licked the walls. Still hooked, still dragged, Gnirth felt the fire as they burst through toward the next building in the row.

A scream cut the air. High. Terrified. In the corner of the collapsing building, hunched away from the flames, a teenager waited for the fire to close in.

The building was coming down. The boar still charged. Gnirth freed one hand from the barbs, ignoring the pain as thorns tore loose, and reached past the flames toward the bog.

The water answered. It tore through the air, hit the young man hard enough to coat him and punch a hole in the wall behind him. The teenager scrambled through as the supports gave way.

Still attached, the boar ablaze and failing, Gnirth felt it drive toward the next building. Before it reached it, the creature buckled. Legs gone. One last squeal, then silence, flames taking what remained.

---

## Into the mist

Across the fields the fog drew closer, dust and dirt obscuring the rows of white flowers. Sunlight broke into rainbows through the particles.

Sabriel reached the edge of the smoke wall. The ground around him was sodden, soaked in black ooze that pulsed as if alive. He took a breath and passed through.

Cold mist breached his skin, seeped into armor, chilled him. Combat sounds dampened. Screaming, crying, explosions faded to white noise. Deeper in, chanting prayers that steadied him, he saw shadows and visages of battle. Broken earth. Briefly a body appeared, then vanished into the mist again. Ghost of the fight, or something worse.

Through the fog, motions in the wind marked where Glasswing Moths hovered, iridescent wings catching what light got through. Sabriel struck first. His sword scratched one glassy body. The damage was slight. The shell was tougher than it looked. Faith kindled light along the blade. A beacon. Still steadied by prayer and quiet chanting.

---

## The warehouse defense

At the warehouses the guards had moved fast. While the creatures were busy with the fight, over half the flower stock had been shifted. A small win against bad odds, and it mattered.

Fianna sent tiny fireflies swarming the Bloodwings and stag, bioluminescence warding and wounding. They took down two of the flying demons in bursts of ichor and chitin.

Snicket arrived and bound the stag in shadow, holding it off the warehouse for a stretch. A Bloodwing dove at Fianna and missed, proboscis wide by inches. Fern's lightning bolt hit the one hovering above. Thunder, then an explosion of remains that coated Fianna in bile and guts.

The fog crept toward the warehouse. With the stag still bound, Fern set a hand on its midriff. It stopped thrashing, as if accepting what came next. A held breath. Then a jolt to the heart that stopped it in a blink. The body went limp. Corruption receded in death.

---

## The vision and the awakening

Gnirth, steady again after the boar, headed for the fog wall to help Sabriel. Farmland had sunk a few inches into black ooze. It wrapped his ankles and pulled with a hunger that felt almost aware.

One step into the rainbow mist and his link to Fern cut out. The Fungril network went silent. Something else took its place.

The visions came.

>[!Quote] Vision
>The ooze feels warm, almost alive, against your skin. For a moment, you're back in Tideveil Enclave. You stand in a circle of your clan, chanting words you can't remember but feel in your bones. The air shimmers with water magic, and before you, a massive water elemental, bound and thrashing. Someone hands you a pouch of dark dust. "For renewal," they whisper. "To cleanse the stagnation." You pour it into the ritual circle, and the elemental screams.

The memory hit hard. Gnirth staggered. What he had done, what he had been, washed through him in horror and recognition.

Ahead, Sabriel's sword glowed faint through the mist. Glasswing Moths drifted toward him, shimmering like broken glass. The mist wall reached the warehouse. A roar ripped the air, primal and ancient, rage and corruption together.

Glasswing Moths shattered where they floated and fell like snow, crystalline bodies breaking apart. The mist thinned to low fog across the fields.

In the middle of the field, it stood.

---

## The warden emerges

A towering antlered spirit of corrupted woods, over twelve feet tall. Twisted roots, bone, and living wood pulsed with a wrong heartbeat. Branch-like antlers crowned it, dripping corrosive sap. Glowing green eyes. Claw-fingers of bone and wood. Roots and branches rearranged as it moved. Spectral ravens circled the antlers, eyes lit the same green.

Around it, a brood of Brambleweb Crawlers. Spider-like insects the size of small dogs, mottled brown-black with sickly green fungal patches. Thorned legs glistened with dark liquid. Red eyes. Mandibles dripping something that ate metal. They moved fast and together, leaving sticky web trails that glowed faint and seemed to decay what they touched.

The party closed on Sabriel and Gnirth. Weapons ready. This was the guardian the abandoned fields had birthed.

---

## The desperate battle

Spiders aimed at Fianna and Fern and missed. The Leshen did not. Venom Thorn Surge swept down on Sabriel. Thorns punched through armor. He avoided the poison, but his chest piece cracked under the blow.

Fianna shifted into a snake and worked with Snicket to poison everything in range. Direct hits on all of them. Spiders took real damage. The Leshen looked as if it enjoyed the venom, corruption feeding on it.

Gnirth reached for water and found only the black ooze soaking the farms. He wielded it anyway. A critical hit tore through the Leshen's torso. The creature staggered back fifteen feet. Vines rose from the ground to brace it as it fell.

Sabriel spent the last of Unstoppable, pouncing to pin the Leshen deeper into tainted soil.

Fern joined him, channeling lightning down through the Leshen and out through his whirlwind. Current arced through wood into earth and thinned the spider horde. Several crawlers convulsed and smoked.

The spiders kept coming. Fangs sank into Fianna for half her health. The venom burned. She cried out.

Damage on the Leshen stacked. It called spectral ravens for distraction. Only one answered. The corruption had weakened even that bond.

The raven hit Fianna and left her vulnerable. Before it could press the opening, her fire ability blew it apart and coated her in more guts. She downed a health potion, cool against burning wounds.

Crowded together, the party gave the spiders an opening. Webs sealed the ground around them. Two of them stuck, webs cutting skin as they struggled and took more damage.

Gnirth poured fire through Sabriel again. Flames tore the Leshen and seared the ground. The webbing burned off. The trapped two came free.

Snicket's shadow blades finished the last spiders in one sweep. Each body that dropped drew a roar from the Leshen, as if it took the hit itself. The bond ran deeper than they had thought. Now only the wooden monstrosity remained.

---

## The final stand

Near death and stripped of its brood, the Leshen turned ferocious. Venom Thorn Surge again, square into Sabriel's cracked chest piece. Severe poison. Strength failing as venom burned through him.

At the same time it called roots from the soil. Everyone but Fern was latched. Roots wrapped appendages and dragged them down. The ground itself had turned.

Fern chanted over the noise, Hex locked on the Leshen. She drowned out the party's pain, drew a breath, and spoke the binding into being. Silent shadow marked the weak points.

Then she struck.

One last lightning bolt, aimed at whatever passed for its heart. Thunder. An intake of breath. It fell for good.

Silence for a breath. The Leshen's body crackled as it crumbled. Corruption pulled back like a tide leaving shore.

Skirmishes at the forest's edge faded soon after. Creatures in retreat. The assault had failed. The cost was still being counted.

---

## Aftermath

Sabriel began gathering bodies from the field and carrying them to the tent. Too many that should not have been there. Farm workers. Guards. Civilians. He spotted Quartermaster Brill tending his own wounds. A nod between soldiers. Sabriel prayed for the fallen and for himself, seeking to cleanse faint-divinity corruption from his body.

Fianna and Fern cleaned off the fluids coating them and collected samples. Two intact Bloodwing beaks. Three sacks of blood. Small shards of Glasswing Moths. Answers, maybe, for later.

Backup took several days to arrive and help with cleanup. The land was tainted. Black ooze deep in the soil. New seed unlikely for months, if ever. The white-flower fields would lie fallow.

Bodies were being returned to Haven for rites. The party's work had recovered the immediate red Lady's Veil supply for processing. No shortage in the near term. Lives saved in the barracks and civilian quarters as well.

They rested, asked around, gathered what they could for the road. The session ended there, victory and loss both written in tainted fields and bodies carried away. Alula waited ahead, and with it Theodore, the Fanewraith, and a corruption that kept spreading.
